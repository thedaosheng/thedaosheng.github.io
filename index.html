01 — Now

C Robotics 仓颉创新 When a field robot gets stuck, an operator in our cockpit takes over — seeing, moving and feeling as if they were there — finishes the job, and that correction becomes training data. Founded April 2026.

The problem

Robots are taught by people who can't feel what the robot touches. I watched one wipe a whiteboard for an hour and never get it clean. Bad teachers make bad policies.

The bet

Foundation models already know a lot about the world. What's missing is learning by doing: on-policy correction from a human who is fully present in the robot's body.

The product

A cockpit that plugs into any arm + torso + base robot: whole-body control, remote force feedback, fingertip touch, and a pipeline that turns every takeover into post-training data.

01 · Body-agnostic

Control that optimizes for the operator's intuition

Most teams treat teleop as a control problem: make the robot track the hand. We think the bottleneck is the human. When the view lags the head, or the body moves in a way the operator didn't expect, people get motion-sick and slow down. So the objective of our whole-body controller is what the operator expects to see and feel:

  • View
    Predict head motion and render ahead, so the picture keeps up with the head — no motion sickness.
  • Body
    Distribute motion the way a person does — arm, then waist, then shoulders, then base — solved jointly, so the robot turns and reaches in one motion.
  • Gaze
    Orbit around the point the operator is looking at, so they can keep working while moving.
  • Portable
    A same-topology adapter: swap the robot, keep the cockpit.

02 · Force

Remote force feedback at 200 ms

Position-only teleop either crushes the object or never quite reaches it. Local force loops feel great but can't leave the lab. We close the force loop across the network.

At 200 ms of latency — roughly Beijing to Washington, D.C. — the remote arm still adjusts its contact force to follow the operator's hand, steadily, so a human can correct a policy mid-rollout from anywhere.

  • Latency200 ms, emulated in the lab
  • LoopOperator force → robot contact force, continuously
  • UnlocksWiping, scraping, inserting, twisting — tasks that need touch
03 · Touch

A display for the fingertip

Force tells you how hard. Touch tells you what. Our tactile display sits on the fingertip and combines three channels in one device:

  • NormalPressing force into the surface
  • ShearSliding force along the surface
  • TextureVibration at 50, 100 and 300 Hz — the bands the skin's fast-adapting receptors respond to

The operator can tell materials apart and feel the exact moment contact switches from hard to soft — the reflex a robot needs to learn.

04 · Learn

From one takeover to a learned skill

Task: squeegee a glass pane. The base policy only replays trajectories, so it skims the glass. An operator takes over and does what the policy never learned — push up to the top edge first, press in, keep contact all the way down. The model learns that key contact event and then does it on its own.
